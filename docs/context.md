# LEERobot SO-101 Workshop Context

## Overview
This project is a 4-hour robotics workshop focused on introducing young participants to robotics, programming, and problem-solving through the practical assembly and first control of a LEERobot SO-101 robotic arm. The workshop combines mechanics, electronics, and programming in a single hands-on session.

## Main objective
Introduce participants to practical robotics by building a complete open-source SO-101 robotic arm from the electronics to the first software-based control, using a real-world assembly process similar to those used in research and robotics laboratories.

## Specific learning objectives
- Mechanical assembly: assemble a 5-degree-of-freedom robotic arm (+ gripper) from 3D-printed parts following a real-world assembly process.
- Basic electronics: wire and connect smart servomotors (Feetech STS3215) in series on a serial communication bus.
- Control and movement: calibrate joints and move the robot arm for the first time using software from a computer via USB; understand concepts such as movement range, torque, and position.
- Introduction to AI in robotics: understand why this arm is used in AI research through the LeRobot library from Hugging Face, opening the door to imitation learning and other applied robotics topics.
- Teamwork and problem solving: participants work in groups of 4-5 to coordinate assembly and debug problems together.
- Real-world robotics practice: participants interact with actual robotic hardware, not just simulations.

## Target participants
- Age: 15 to 35 years
- Group size: 8-10 people
- Previous level: no prior requirements; all levels are welcome
- Duration: 4 hours (single session)

## Learning outcomes
By the end of the workshop, participants should be able to:
- Assemble and wire a SO-101 robot from scratch
- Understand the basics of servomotors and movement control
- Program simple movement sequences

The initiative aims to bring robotics and technology vocations closer to young people in Andorra through a practical, accessible format that is usually found mainly at the university level.

## Hardware: LEERobot SO-101
- 6-DOF robotic arm (total), designed for education
- 5 DOF + 1 gripper
- Feetech STS3215 motors, configurable and suitable for robotics control
- Estimated assembly time: 1-1.5 hours per arm for a team of 4 people

## Workshop structure (4 hours)
The workshop is approximately 10% theory and 90% practice.

1. Welcome + Theory (10 min)
   - Presentation of workshop objectives
   - Explanation of robotics, programming, and problem-solving concepts
   - Demo video of the SO-101 in operation
   - Safety rules and workshop usage guidance

2. Physical Assembly (60 min)
   - Participants assemble the robotic arms in teams of 4-5
   - Ongoing supervision by instructors
   - Motors are pre-configured and calibrated; parts have already been checked in a preliminary assembly step

3. Verification (10 min)
   - Functional testing of each arm
   - Verification of sensors and actuators
   - Troubleshooting if needed

4. Programming + Practical Challenges (90 min)
   - Teams rotate through 1-2 challenge rounds of 45 minutes each
   - Challenge 1: program the arm to pick up an object from an initial position and place it in a final position, and vice versa
   - Challenge 2: program the arm to assemble and disassemble 3-4 blocks

5. Robotic Jenga (60 min)
   - Teams build and dismantle a tower inspired by Jenga or lightweight blocks
   - The arm must pick a specific piece and place it on top of the tower without causing it to fall
   - Programming required: move to coordinates (x, y, z), open/close gripper, return home
   - Scoring: +1 for each successful move, -2 if the tower falls
   - Tie-breaker rules apply based on speed and progress made

6. Closing (10 min)
   - Final demo
   - Awards and conclusion

## Key theme
The session is designed as a practical introduction to robotics, not only as a theoretical lecture. The core experience is building, testing, and controlling a real robot in a collaborative and playful environment.

## Usefulness for a project context
This workshop is a hands-on educational robotics initiative intended to:
- expose participants to real robot hardware
- connect robotics with coding and experimentation
- encourage team collaboration and troubleshooting
- create an accessible bridge between STEM education and applied AI/robotics research
